# Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Model Reasoning: A Comprehensive Survey

## TL;DR
- Reinforcement learning (RL) paradigms for LLM reasoning range from implicit preference optimization (such as DPO) to online policy gradient methods (such as PPO), with recent systematic studies showing that PPO outperforms DPO by up to 2.5% on mathematical reasoning benchmarks when hyperparameters and reference model updates are properly tuned [1].
- Step-by-step verification via process reward models (PRMs) and Monte Carlo Tree Search (e.g., ReST-MCTS* and OmegaPRM) successfully scales automated process supervision and data collection without human annotators [2][3], although recent multi-domain evaluations reveal that generative outcome reward models (GenORM) can rival or surpass PRMs by avoiding error compounding on long reasoning trajectories [4].
- Reward shaping techniques such as Thickening-to-Thinning (T2T) dynamically modulate response length incentives according to model competence, outperforming standard GRPO on competitive mathematical benchmarks like MATH-500, AIME, and AMC [5].
- Scaling test-time compute through hybrid parallel-sequential frameworks (such as SETS, Forest-of-Thought, and step-level PRM-guided MCTS) substantially improves reasoning efficiency [6][7][8], while verifier gains depend critically on cross-family divergence rather than self-verification [9].

## Background
Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a cornerstone for post-training Large Language Models (LLMs) to enhance complex multi-step reasoning, mathematical problem-solving, and code generation. Traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) endows models with syntactic fluency and basic instruction-following capabilities, but often falls short on tasks requiring extended chains of thought, self-correction, and rigorous multi-hop logical deduction. To bridge this gap, reinforcement learning algorithms align model generation distributions with scalar rewards or preference feedback.

The foundational paradigm of RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) relies on training an explicit reward model to score generated responses, followed by policy optimization via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). However, online PPO requires maintaining multiple model instances (actor, critic, reference policy, and reward model) and is notoriously sensitive to hyperparameter choices. This complexity motivated offline, preference-based alternatives like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), which parameterizes the reward implicitly in terms of the policy and reference model, enabling closed-form extraction of the optimal policy via a simple binary cross-entropy loss [10].

Despite the popularity of implicit preference optimization, recent research underscores a persistent tension between static preference alignment and active exploration required for reasoning. Complex reasoning tasks—such as competitive programming and mathematical theorem proving—demand extensive search, credit assignment across intermediate reasoning steps, and robust handling of out-of-distribution (OOD) generation paths. Consequently, modern research has branched into four active directions: foundational preference optimization trade-offs, process-supervised reward modeling and tree search, reward shaping and competence-aware incentives, and inference-time test-time compute scaling.

## Preference Optimization Foundations: PPO vs. DPO in Reasoning Tasks
The debate between online reinforcement learning (PPO) and offline preference optimization (DPO) has intensified as researchers evaluate their efficacy on reasoning benchmarks. While DPO bypasses explicit reward model training and online sampling, comprehensive empirical analyses reveal fundamental limitations [11]. Specifically, DPO is susceptible to out-of-distribution exploitation and high sensitivity to distribution shifts between the base model outputs and offline preference data [11].

Disentangling the best practices for learning from preference feedback demonstrates that preference data quality exerts the largest influence on downstream performance (up to an 8% improvement), followed closely by algorithm selection [1]. When hyperparameters such as advantage normalization, large batch sizes, and exponential moving average (EMA) updates for the reference model are carefully tuned, PPO consistently outperforms vanilla DPO by up to 2.5% in mathematics and 1.2% in general domains [1]. Furthermore, models optimized via PPO exhibit a significantly higher propensity to spontaneously generate structured chain-of-thought reasoning when prompted with mathematical and logical tasks [1].

To harness the complementary strengths of both paradigms, hybrid frameworks such as Mixed Preference Optimization (MPO) combine offline preference training with online reinforcement learning [12]. MPO trains an initial DPO model on curated preference data to establish a robust baseline policy, which then serves as both the policy and reference model during subsequent PPO training on difficult reasoning tasks [12]. This two-stage procedure avoids the performance degradation commonly observed in vanilla DPO while retaining stable online exploration [12]. Concurrently, unified frameworks like Reasoning-Aligned Reinforcement Learning (RARL) categorize reward mechanisms across RLHF, DPO, RLAIF, and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), highlighting that implicit reward models in DPO struggle with accurate credit assignment over multi-step trajectories due to policy entanglement and reference model uncertainty [13].

## Process Supervision, Outcome Verification, and Search-Based RL
Because multi-step reasoning chains can fail at any intermediate logical link, recent research has heavily emphasized step-level verification over sparse outcome-level supervision. Automated process supervision frameworks, such as OmegaPRM and ReST-MCTS*, leverage tree search algorithms and binary search heuristics to identify the first logical error in reasoning traces without manual human annotation [2][3]. In ReST-MCTS*, Monte Carlo Tree Search guides the collection of high-quality reasoning traces and estimates per-step values by evaluating the probability that an intermediate step leads to the final correct answer [2]. This inferred process reward serves a dual purpose: updating process reward models (PRMs) and curating high-performing trajectories for iterative policy self-training, consistently outperforming Best-of-N and Tree-of-Thought baselines [2].

Similarly, OmegaPRM utilizes a divide-and-conquer Monte Carlo Tree Search strategy to collect over 1.5 million automated process supervision annotations [3]. When combined with weighted self-consistency, this automated process supervision achieved a 69.4% success rate on the MATH benchmark for Gemini Pro, representing a 36% relative improvement over the 51% base model [3].

Despite the theoretical appeal of fine-grained supervision, recent multi-domain evaluations challenge the universal superiority of process reward models [4]. A systematic study comparing discriminative and generative outcome reward models (DisORM, DisPRM, GenORM, GenPRM) across 14 diverse domains found that generative outcome reward models (GenORM) are remarkably robust and frequently outperform PRMs [4]. Stepwise process scoring often inherits label noise from auto-labeling LLMs and accumulates errors as reasoning trajectories lengthen, whereas outcome-based generative scoring circumvents error compounding in long-horizon reasoning [4].

## Reward Shaping and Competence-Aware Exploration
To prevent models from collapsing into premature convergence or generating excessively verbose, redundant chains of thought during RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards), advanced reward shaping strategies have been developed [5]. Competence-aware reward shaping schemes such as Thickening-to-Thinning (T2T) dynamically modulate response length incentives according to the model's running success probability on specific problem types [5].

When a model is deemed incompetent and struggles to solve a given task, T2T incentivizes longer reasoning trajectories ("thickening") on incorrect attempts, thereby broadening the search space and encouraging the exploration of novel solution paths [5]. Conversely, upon achieving correctness, the framework applies length penalties ("thinning") to suppress redundancy, foster model confidence, and distill concise reasoning capabilities [5]. Empirical evaluations show that T2T substantially outperforms standard Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) across competitive mathematical benchmarks including MATH-500, AIME, and AMC [5].

## Test-Time Compute Scaling and Hybrid Inference Frameworks
Scaling inference-time compute has emerged as an orthogonal yet highly synergistic paradigm to reinforcement learning. Rather than relying solely on static model weights, test-time compute allocation leverages parallel sampling, sequential self-correction, and tree search against verifiers [6][14][7][8]. Frameworks such as Self-Enhanced Test-Time Scaling (SETS) integrate sampling, self-verification, and self-correction into a unified architecture, achieving superior scaling efficiency across planning, math, and coding benchmarks (such as LiveBench, MATH-500, and AIME) with less performance degradation than standalone repeated sampling or iterative refinement [6].

Compute-optimal scaling strategies further demonstrate that the efficacy of inference-time algorithms depends critically on prompt difficulty [14]. Under FLOPs-matched evaluations, adaptive test-time compute allocation enables smaller base models to outperform models up to 14x larger on non-trivial problem distributions [14]. For simpler tasks, revision models and intra-family self-refinement are effective, whereas difficult problems require parallel search and tree search over high-level strategies guided by process reward models [14].

To execute structured tree search at test time, the Forest-of-Thought (FoT) framework integrates multiple reasoning trees using sparse activation strategies alongside rule-based mathematical matching functions (such as detecting division by zero or symbol misuse) to catch errors in real time [7]. Similarly, step-level verifier-guided hybrid scaling combines Best-of-N sampling, conditional process-based self-refinement, and MCTS, using heuristic halting rules (e.g., skipping reflection when PRM confidence exceeds 0.9) to eliminate unnecessary computational overhead [8].

## Verifier Generalization and Cross-Family Evaluation Dynamics
A critical bottleneck in verification-guided RL and test-time search is the susceptibility of reward models and verifiers to exploitation and false positives [9]. Investigating solution verifiers reveals that "verifier gain"—the expected performance improvement from rejection sampling—depends heavily on the distribution distance between the solver and the verifier [9].

Self-verification yields the lowest verifier gains due to shared biases and high false positive rates when accepting incorrect solver outputs [9]. Intra-family verification provides moderate gains, while cross-family verification offers the greatest potential performance improvements [9]. Furthermore, while post-training enhances a model's intrinsic generation capabilities, it often diminishes its self-verification utility by increasing false positive rates, highlighting the necessity of decoupling solver and verifier populations in reinforcement learning pipelines [9].

## Trends and Open Problems
- **Bridging Offline Preference Optimization and Online Exploration:** While DPO and its variants offer computational simplicity, developing robust offline-to-online algorithms that mitigate out-of-distribution preference exploitation remains a critical open problem.
- **Mitigating Error Accumulation in Long Trajectories:** Resolving the tension between process supervision (PRMs) and outcome supervision (ORMs) to prevent error compounding over extended reasoning chains is vital for scaling reasoning in complex scientific and mathematical domains.
- **Compute-Optimal Test-Time Allocation:** Designing adaptive allocation mechanisms that dynamically balance parallel sampling, multi-step tree search, and step-level verification based on real-time problem complexity continues to be an active frontier.
- **Cross-Family Verifier Generalization:** Addressing self-enhancement bias in verifiers by constructing independent, cross-family verification systems will be essential to maximize rejection sampling and search efficiency.
