# Comprehensive Survey on World Models: Foundations, Video Simulation, and Open Challenges

## TL;DR
- World models serve dual philosophical and functional paradigms: understanding environmental dynamics via latent abstractions (e.g., Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures) and predicting future trajectories through generative video simulation [1].
- Foundational RL systems like DreamerV3 unify continuous and discrete control across 150 diverse domains using Recurrent State-Space Models (RSSM) and symlog predictions [2], while early architectures like Ha & Schmidhuber (2018) demonstrated autonomous agents training successfully inside internal hallucinated dreams [3].
- Large-scale generative video simulators—such as OpenAI's Sora [4], Genie's unsupervised latent action models [5], and DIAMOND's score-based diffusion model for Atari [6]—demonstrate emergent 3D geometric consistency, stable multi-step image-space rollouts, and strong interactive simulation capabilities.
- Embodied applications in robotics and autonomous driving leverage predictive multiview forecasting (e.g., Drive-WM) [7] and latent simulators to improve sample efficiency [8], though long-horizon rollouts frequently suffer from kinematic-only consistency rather than true physical dynamics [9].

## Background
The paradigm of world models in machine learning and reinforcement learning traces its roots to cognitive science and control theory, where an intelligent agent constructs an internal representation of its environment to anticipate the consequences of actions without requiring constant direct interaction. Early computational realizations, notably the framework introduced by Ha & Schmidhuber (2018), decoupled spatial perception from temporal dynamics [3]. By combining an unsupervised Convolutional Variational Autoencoder (ConvVAE) spatial encoder with a Mixture Density Network and Recurrent Neural Network (MDN-RNN) temporal dynamics model, agents could be trained entirely inside their own hallucinated neural dreams and successfully transferred back to physical environments [3].

Over subsequent years, the field expanded into two distinct yet complementary perspectives: understanding the world through abstract representation spaces and predicting the future through explicit generative simulation [1]. Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) advanced significantly with Recurrent State-Space Models (RSSM), culminating in scalable systems like DreamerV3, which handles variable reward scales and multi-domain tasks via categorical latent representations and symlog scaling [2]. Simultaneously, self-supervised learning shifted toward non-generative representation learning through architectures such as I-JEPA, which predicts missing target block representations in abstract space rather than reconstructing raw pixels [10]. More recently, the scaling of transformer and diffusion architectures has blurred the boundary between generative video models and interactive neural game engines [6][4].

## Foundational Paradigms and Representation Architectures
Foundational world models rely on compact state spaces to capture environmental transitions. In DreamerV3, environmental interactions are modeled via an RSSM that incorporates categorical latent variables and KL balancing to prevent representational collapse [2]. To maintain stability across drastically different reward scales and environments—spanning continuous control, discrete actions, visual inputs, and 3D simulation such as Minecraft—DreamerV3 adopts symlog predictions [2]. This formulation avoids hand-tuned reward clipping while stabilizing policy gradients.

In parallel, non-generative self-supervised frameworks address the computational expense and potential hallucination of pixel-level reconstruction. The Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (I-JEPA) predicts the representation of unseen image target blocks directly from context blocks using an asymmetric Vision Transformer (ViT) [10]. By operating in an abstract representation space and updating the target encoder via an exponential moving average, I-JEPA avoids representation collapse without relying on hand-crafted data augmentations or pixel-level decoders [10]. Comprehensive taxonomies categorize these evolving paradigms into latent abstraction models and direct generative forecasting frameworks [1].

## Video Generation and Interactive Simulation Models
The scaling hypothesis has driven a renaissance in video generation models functioning as world simulators. OpenAI's Sora combines Spacetime Patch Diffusion Transformers (DiT) with spatial-temporal VAE compressors to ingest raw videos of variable resolutions and durations [4]. By breaking videos down into spacetime patches analogous to text tokens and leveraging DALL·E 3-style detailed video re-captioning, Sora exhibits emergent 3D geometric consistency, long-range object permanence, and multi-camera consistency [4].

For interactive control, Genie introduces an unsupervised foundation world model (11B total parameters, 10.1B dynamics model) trained on 30,000 hours of 2D platformer gameplay videos without action labels or text annotations [5]. Genie utilizes a VQ-VAE video tokenizer, a causal latent action model (LAM) to infer continuous latent actions between frames, and a MaskGIT transformer dynamics model to enable frame-by-frame user control [5]. In reinforcement learning benchmarks, DIAMOND (Diffusion As a Model Of eNvironment Dreams) implements a score-based diffusion model (EDM) with a 2D U-Net backbone for Atari 100k, achieving robust long-horizon stability with low denoising step counts ($n=3$ NFE) and scaling up to 381M parameters on static CS:GO gameplay [6]. Furthermore, open-source initiatives like Open-Sora 1.2 democratize efficient video generation via 1.1B parameter Spatial-Temporal Diffusion Transformers (STDiT) and 384M-parameter 3D causal VAE compressors, evaluated systematically using VBench [11].

## Embodied AI, Robotics, and Autonomous Driving Applications
World models serve as crucial predictive engines and latent simulators for embodied artificial intelligence, particularly in robotics and autonomous driving [8][7]. In robotic manipulation and navigation, world models mitigate sample complexity bottlenecks by allowing policies to learn from imagined rollouts and synthetic trajectories [8]. For autonomous driving, frameworks such as Drive-WM generate high-fidelity multiview visual forecasting to evaluate future risks and plan trajectories prior to executing physical control actions [7].

Despite these capabilities, rigorous evaluation remains challenging. In autonomous driving benchmarking (e.g., using CARLA and the nuScenes dataset), open-loop planning evaluations using human trajectory alignment often fail to correlate with closed-loop task success [12]. Furthermore, closed-loop simulation benchmarks are highly sensitive to random seeds, necessitating multi-seed evaluations to establish statistically robust performance [12].

## Trends and Open Problems
Current research highlights critical limitations in long-horizon world-model rollouts. Rather than generic compounding error causing failure, recent diagnostic protocols reveal that imagined rollouts tend to be kinematically consistent rather than dynamically faithful [9]. Using Imagined Kinematic-Consistency Error (iKCE) and friction-sweep perturbations, studies demonstrate that world model rollouts remain statistically invariant across physical regime boundaries (such as gait-collapse thresholds) even when task rewards collapse [9].

Open problems in the field include bridging the gap between kinematic forecasting and genuine physical dynamics modeling, improving out-of-distribution robustness, reducing the computational overhead of high-resolution diffusion and transformer rollouts, and establishing standardized closed-loop evaluation standards that transcend open-loop perceptual metrics.
